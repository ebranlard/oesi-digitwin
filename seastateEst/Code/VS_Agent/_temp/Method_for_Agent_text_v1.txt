1 Notes on Kalman-Filters

A Kalman filter allows for the estimation of variables that are not physically being
measured, while filtering out noise from sensors. It combines information from two
imperfect sources:

   1. The physics model (Prediction): The error level Q of the physics model depends
      on the choice of simplifications. Example: Wind speed is assumed to be steady

   2. The sensors (Measurements): The error level R of the measurements depends on
      the level of noise in the data. The noise can come from various sources such as
      temperature, electrical noise, etc.

The Kalman filter predicts the systems’state’s using the physics model and corrects its
estimate using the real measurements. It uses only the measurements from the current
and previous time step. This process is repeated recursively. The covariance matrix P of
the filter determines how much weight the physics model and estimate each have. The
covariance matrix tracks the uncertainty and cross-correlation within the states. As an
example:

   1. If a floating platform has been tracking smoothly for an hour, P will be very small.
      The past estimate is likely very good, therefore a noisy sensor reading won’t tip
      the digital twin’s estimate.

   2. If a floating platform was just it by a large unexpected wave which the physics
      model couldn’t predict, P will spike. The Kalman filter will now de-value the past
      estimate and rely more on the incoming sensor measurements.

2.1.1 Retrieving M from an OpenFAST .lin file

A wind turbine system modeled in OpenFAST is governed by the structural equations of
motion:


                           M (q)q̈ + C(q, q̇)q̇ + K(q)q = F (q, q̇, q̈)                  (5)

Where:

   • q, q̇, q̈: The generalized structural coordinates, velocities, and accelerations.

   • M (q): The global mass matrix (containing structural mass and hydrodynamic
     added mass A∞ ).

   • F : The vector of external forces (aerodynamic, hydrodynamic, mooring, and control
     inputs u).

Linearization in OpenFAST looks at small deviations (perturbations denoted by ∆)
around a steady operating point. If we isolate just the forces applied directly to the
platform (∆uptfm ) and look at the immediate acceleration response (∆q̈ptfm ), the stiffness

(K) and damping (C) terms drop out because they require time to pass or positions to
change before they can generate forces. Eq. (5) becomes:


                               Mptf m · ∆q̈ptf m = ∆uptf m                             (6)

To solve for the accelerations, we multiply both sides by the inverse mass matrix:


                               ∆q̈ptf m = Mptf
                                           −1
                                               m · ∆uptf m                             (7)

In OpenFAST the continuous state-space equation for the state derivatives is:


                                   ∆ẋ = A∆x + B∆u                                     (8)

If we assume the system is sitting exactly at its operating point for a split second, the
state perturbation is zero (∆x = 0). The equation reduces to:


                                       ∆ẋ = B∆u                                       (9)

If we strictly isolate the rows for platform velocity state derivatives (∆q̈ptfm ) and the
columns for platform force inputs (∆uptfm ), we get our specific 6x6 Bsubset :


                               ∆q̈ptf m = Bsubset · ∆uptf m                           (10)

By comparing Eq. (7) with Eq. (10) we find that the Bsubset matrix mathematically is
the inverse mass matrix:

2.4 Radiation convolution integral

The radiation force is a result of the structure displacing its surrounding water as it
moves, creating waves that radiate away from the body. It is expressed as an added mass
force depended on the added mass at infinite frequency A∞ , as well as a convolution of
the radiation impulse response function Krad (t), also known as the radiation kernel, and
the body velocity:


                                                 Z t
                      Frad (t) = −A∞ q̈(t) −            Krad (t − τ )q̇(τ )dτ        (42)
                                                    0


Here τ is the integration variable representing past time. For a six degree of freedom
platform, Krad (t) is a 6x6 matrix. We move the added mass term to the left-hand side of
Cummins equation. The total mass matrix becomes a summation of the added mass and
structural mass matrices:



                                        M = Ms + A∞                                  (43)
